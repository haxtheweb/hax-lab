SIIP, the Sustainable Impact and Innovation Pipeline, turns a course into a contributor pipeline for an open source project without turning the course into "the project class". Students learn the discipline. The project supplies real problems. It's how HAX kept shipping with one full-time developer, and it works at other schools and for other projects.

Why it exists

HAX went from a note card in 2015 to four full-time people at its peak, then lost developers to industry after the pandemic until one remained in 2023. Feature output still climbed sharply from 2024, because the classroom became a contributor pipeline. HAX 26 shipped the day before the talk that introduced SIIP.

How a term is shaped

A SIIP course spends its time in four parts. The onboarding on this site follows the same shape.

Make thinking visible

The teaching method is cognitive apprenticeship: the way a trade is learned, adapted for work you can't see someone doing.

The instructor works on the real codebase out loud, narrating decisions and not only steps.

Students do the same work with support: starter code, checklists, recorded walkthroughs.

Support drops away as skill grows, until students work from an issue alone.

Task choice, hints, feedback and rising challenge, in class and in code review.

What it has taught us

  • The biggest gains come six to twelve months after students take the class.
  • The top 5 to 10% of a class often choose to go further.
  • HAX is the backdrop, not the subject. A gap in the docs becomes a contribution, so fixing docs is part of onboarding.
  • Visible credit matters. Students' work is posted publicly, with their permission.
  • Never make contributing to one project a grade students can't opt out of. Offer another path.
