Faculty fund student work that solves their own problems, and HAX gains the capability for everyone. The faculty get what they need, the students get real, funded work, and the result ships in HAX. Grant sizes aren't listed here on purpose: it isn't a competition, and this work doesn't need much money.

Faculty who have secured funding for the lab

  • Bryan Ollendyke, College of Information Sciences and Technology. HAX's project lead teaches the web development course the lab grew out of, and has secured funding for lab work.
  • Dr. Blake Colaianne, Human Development and Family Studies. Director of the Learn to Flourish Hub, whose public site runs on HAX at flourish.hhd.psu.edu, and has secured funding for lab work.
  • Dr. Jennifer Frank: the International Student Portfolio. She funded portfolios international students build and take with them. HAX grew portfolio support from it, and site skeletons came straight from its needs. Because students build it for other students, she says, she never has to argue that it matters.
  • Dr. David Fusco: Canvas AI analysis. Seven or eight students, through his grant and internships, built a HAX command line tool that pulls a Canvas course through its API, reports how ready its assignments are for AI and how easily AI could be used to cheat on them, then launches a live HAX site from the result.

Bring the lab a problem

What do your students or your unit need that HAX can't do yet? Real problems make the best lab projects.

A grant or an internship pays students to build it. They learn on real work, and you get a result you can keep using.

Teaching web or software development? SIIP shows how to point a course at an open source project, and faculty resources in PRAW collect the rest.
